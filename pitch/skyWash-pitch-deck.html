The problem

In Nigerian cities, laundry is a weekly necessity stuck in an offline, high-friction system.

People don’t hate laundry shops. They hate the time, traffic, uncertainty, and lack of trust around getting clothes cleaned.

Time tax

Drop-off, wait, pickup — often across bad traffic. Busy workers and students simply don’t have spare hours.

Trust gap

Unclear pricing, no tracking, inconsistent quality. Informal helpers don’t scale; premium services cost too much.

Fragmented supply

Neighborhood laundries have capacity but weak digital reach. Demand exists — distribution is broken.

Why now: denser cities, mobile payments, and expectation of on-demand services2 / 12

Why Nigeria · Why now

A massive urban convenience market with offline laundry at the center of weekly life.

Market reality

  • Rapid urbanization + long commute culture
  • Mobile-first consumers already used to on-demand apps
  • Paystack/card & transfer rails make digital collection workable
  • Thousands of local washers need demand aggregation

Our wedge

  • Start with one dense community (estate / campus / office)
  • Own reliability + repeat weekly routes
  • Expand neighborhood-by-neighborhood
  • Then unlock B2B (short-lets, salons, small hotels)
We win on density and trust — not national ads on day one3 / 12
